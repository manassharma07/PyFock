# DF_algo=12: multipole-accelerated density fitting

`DF_algo=12` evaluates the density-fitted Coulomb term with the shell-blocked Rys
integrals of `DF_algo=11` for the *near field* and multipole expansions for the *far
field*, in the spirit of the continuous fast multipole method (White, Johnson, Gill,
Head-Gordon, Chem. Phys. Lett. 230, 8 (1994)) applied to the three-center integrals of
the resolution of the identity. Code: `pyfock/Integrals/df_algo12_helpers.py` (algorithm),
`pyfock/Integrals/df_algo12_helpers_cupy.py` with `df_algo12_cuda_core.py` (CUDA driver) and
`pyfock/Integrals/multipole_helpers.py` (solid harmonics, translations, Gaussian moments).

It is the **default** since PyFock 0.2.1, on the CPU and on the GPU, for pure (non-hybrid)
functionals. Runs that need RI exact exchange (`xc='HF'`, hybrid functionals) fall back to
`DF_algo=11` automatically, since exchange contracts the three-center blocks the far field
replaces.

```python
dft_obj.DF_algo = 12
dft_obj.multipole_options = {'precision': 1e-10, 'lmax': 12, 'box_size': 2.5, 'separation': 4.0,
                             'class_factor': 4.0, 'break_even': 1.0, 'low_memory': False}  # the defaults
```

## 1. What is computed

Density fitting needs, in every SCF iteration,

    gamma_P = sum_ij D_ij (ij|P)          J_ij = sum_P (ij|P) c_P ,

the Coulomb interaction of the product distributions `phi_i phi_j` with the auxiliary
functions `P`. Whenever a distribution and an auxiliary function do not overlap, their
interaction is that of two classical charge distributions and equals a *finite* double
multipole expansion up to an `erfc`-type overlap error. The tensor `(ij|P)` is therefore
split into a near-field part, evaluated exactly, and a far-field part, evaluated through
moments.

**Distributions.** A shell pair is a sum of primitive pair products
`g_a g_b = exp(-p|r - P_ab|^2) x polynomial of degree l_a + l_b` centred at
`P_ab = (alpha A + beta B)/(alpha + beta)`. The primitive pairs of a shell pair are grouped
by centre ("groups"; shells on the same atom share one centre). About its own centre a
group has exact moments only up to `l = l_a + l_b`
(`multipole_helpers.gaussian_product_moments`); they are computed once and stored per
function pair: `(l_a + l_b + 1)^2` doubles per function pair and group, about 1% of the
far-field integrals they replace. Groups are sorted into cubic boxes (`box_size`) and,
inside a box, into extent classes (geometric factor `class_factor`); every (box, class)
is a *branch* whose expansion centre is the midpoint of its members' bounding box.

**Auxiliary functions.** `x^a y^b z^c exp(-q r^2)` on atom `A` has exact moments about
`A` only up to `l = a + b + c` (in SAO mode only `l = l_shell`). The far-field potential
of the fitted density is thus generated by one exact point multipole of order `<= l_aux`
per (atom, auxiliary shell), and no hierarchy is needed on this side.

**Near field.** For a shell pair and an auxiliary shell `K` the Rys kernel evaluates the
primitive pairs whose branch is not well separated from `K`; the block is stored in the
block-sparse layout of `DF_algo=11` (shells for which every primitive pair is far field
are left out of the columns). Schwarz screening, strict pair cut-off, spherical
projection and the `max_memory_ints3c2e` budget are unchanged. Tight core primitive
pairs, which dominate the Rys cost, are compact and become far field for almost every
auxiliary shell even when their shell pair also contains diffuse primitives - which is
why the far-field share of the *work* is roughly twice its share of the *stored
integrals*.

**Profitability.** The multipole path costs `(lmax + 1)^2` operations per function pair and
iteration; the direct contraction costs one per far-field auxiliary function. A group is only
expanded once it has at least `break_even * (lmax + 1)^2` far-field auxiliary functions.
Without this rule, molecules whose far field is a thin shell of distant functions (benzene,
caffeine) pay for expansions that replace almost nothing and end up slower than `DF_algo=11`;
with it they fall back to it instead.

**Pre-translated moments.** Each function pair's moments are translated to its branch centre
*once*, at build time, and stored as `Mtil[entry][r, :]` (one entry per shell pair and branch).
Doing this per iteration instead costs a sparse translation per group per pass, which measured
about three times more than the near-field contraction it was replacing. With the translation
hoisted out, an iteration only needs a dense length-`(lmax + 1)^2` axpy per row for `gamma` and
the matching dot product for `J`. The near-field column map of every pair is precomputed for the
same reason.

**Per iteration.** `gamma`: the density-weighted branch moments accumulate from `Mtil`; the
branch moments are coupled to local expansions (orders `<= l_aux`) at every far-field atom;
`gamma_P` adds the contraction of that local expansion with the moments of `P`. `J`: the exact
point multipoles of the auxiliary shells (weighted by `c_P`) give a local expansion at every
branch centre, which is contracted with `Mtil` directly. The same moments, translations and
coupling tensors are used in both directions (local-to-local is the exact adjoint of
multipole-to-multipole under the same truncation), so `gamma` and `J` derive from one
approximate `(ij|P)` tensor and the energy stays variational.

**Well-separatedness.** Branch `b` (centre `B`, radius `r_b`, most diffuse member
exponent `p_b`, largest member "charge" `Q_b`) and auxiliary shell `K` on atom `A`
(smallest exponent `q_K`, charge `Q_K`) interact through multipoles when

    |A - B| - r_b >= erfc^-1(precision / (Q_b Q_K)) sqrt(1/p_b + 1/q_K)      (overlap)
    |A - B|       >= separation * r_b                                        (truncation)

The overlap line bounds the `erfc`-type deviation of every member's interaction from its
multipole form (`Q_b Q_K erfc(sqrt(pq/(p+q)) d)/d` for two Gaussians at distance `d`);
the "charge" of a Gaussian is its coefficient times the Gaussian-product prefactor and
normalization volume. The truncation line bounds the error of the branch expansions,
which converge like `(r_b/|A - B|)^(lmax+1)`.

## 2. Solid-harmonic toolkit

`multipole_helpers` works with real scaled solid harmonics, `R_lm = r^l P_l^m e^{imphi}/(l+m)!`
and `I_lm = (l-m)! P_l^m e^{imphi}/r^(l+1)` (real combinations, `harmonic_index(l, m) = l^2 + l + m`),
for which `1/|r - a| = sum_lm R_lm(a) I_lm(r)` and the addition theorem
`R_LM(a + b) = sum A^{LM}_{lm,jk} R_lm(a) R_jk(b)` carry no extra coefficients. The sparse real
coefficients `A` (`translation_table`, generated numerically from the complex form and the
complex-to-real transform) drive all three translations, and the branch/atom coupling
tensor is `T_{lm,jk}(R) = (-1)^j sum_M A^{l+j,M}_{lm,jk} I_{l+j,M}(R)`. The kernels are
verified against explicit Legendre functions, point-charge sums and numerical
quadrature in `tests/test_df_algo12.py`.

## 3. Accuracy

With the defaults, fully converged SCF energies (PBE, `conv_crit` 1e-7, same iteration count
for both algorithms) agree with `DF_algo=11` two to three orders of magnitude below that
threshold:

| System (def2-SVP, SAO) | far field int./work | converged E(12) - E(11) |
|---|---|---|
| H2O | 0.0% / 0.0% | -4.3e-14 Ha |
| Benzene | 0.0% / 0.9% | -1.9e-11 Ha |
| Serotonin | 4.1% / 17.7% | +5.2e-10 Ha |
| Caffeine | 4.0% / 17.1% | +7.7e-10 Ha |
| Cholesterol | 32.4% / 44.5% | +4.6e-10 Ha |
| Water chain, 5 molecules 5 A apart | 51.7% / - | +4.0e-12 Ha |

Analytical nuclear gradients are unaffected: `DFT_Grad` reads no density-fitting state and
builds its own three-center derivative integrals, and on the 5-water chain above the gradients
of the two algorithms agree to 3.1e-13 Ha/Bohr on components of order 2.5e-2.

Block by block, `approx_block` against `exact_block` over a random sample of blocks with
far-field content (`tests/test_df_algo12.py` requires better than 2e-9 on a water chain):

| System | max abs error of a block | rms |
|---|---|---|
| Caffeine | 2.2e-11 | 4.8e-13 |
| Cholesterol | 7.0e-12 | 1.9e-13 |

Plan-level sensitivity to the parameters, on caffeine with a converged PBE density
(`dE` = DF Coulomb energy against algorithm 11, `max abs dJ` = largest element of the
Coulomb matrix difference):

| options | far field int./work | dE (Ha) | max abs dJ |
|---|---|---|---|
| defaults (`lmax` 12, `separation` 4) | 4.0% / 17.1% | 1.8e-12 | 1.6e-9 |
| `lmax` 12, `separation` 3 | 4.1% / 17.6% | -1.1e-9 | 5.1e-9 |
| `lmax` 10, `separation` 4 | 4.4% / 17.7% | 9.4e-10 | 3.1e-8 |
| `lmax` 10, `separation` 3 | 4.5% / 18.3% | -1.0e-8 | 6.0e-8 |
| `precision` 1e-8 | 13.5% / 24.8% | 5.2e-9 | 1.0e-7 |
| `precision` 1e-6 | 17.8% / - | 7.4e-7 | 7.9e-6 |

`precision` controls the overlap criterion, `lmax` and `separation` the truncation error. The
two act almost independently of the far-field fraction here: on icosane the fraction is the
same to one decimal for every `lmax` between 6 and 14 and every `separation` between 3 and 5,
because the overlap criterion binds everywhere and the truncation criterion never does. That is
why the defaults buy accuracy through `lmax` and `separation` rather than through `precision`:
they cost storage and arithmetic but almost no far field. `lmax` 10 was tried as a default and
rejected: it gave a converged caffeine error of 1.7e-7 Hartree, above `conv_crit`.

The far-field fraction itself is limited by the diffuse valence functions (pair exponents around
0.3, auxiliary s exponents around 0.2): their multipole approximation only becomes accurate
beyond 9 to 12 bohr. Molecules the size of caffeine (14 bohr across) therefore have almost no
far field, cholesterol (35 bohr) about a third, and extended chains most of it.

def2-universal-jfit, SAO, native level-3 grids, `save_ao_values=False`, three SCF iterations
from the same starting density, fastest of three runs. "3c2e + J" is the three-center build plus
all per-iteration `gamma`/`J` work, which is the part the far field replaces; the two-center
metric is a fixed cost both algorithms pay in full and is listed separately. "DF memory" is the
stored three-center integrals plus the far-field moments.

The crossover is around a molecular extent of 25 to 30 bohr. Below it the far field is a thin
shell of distant auxiliary functions: the break-even rule declines most of it, what remains
replaces a few percent of the stored integrals, and algorithm 12 lands within about 10% of
algorithm 11 without a useful saving. Above it both the time and the memory advantage grow
with the extent, because the fraction of distribution/auxiliary pairs that are well separated
does.

Timings on this machine vary by up to a factor of two between runs of identical work (the
desktop session competes for the same cores), which is why the benchmark takes the fastest of
several runs; the memory figures and the far-field fractions are exact and repeat exactly.

## 5. Memory, and the `low_memory` option

The Coulomb term's storage is the near-field three-center integrals plus the far-field moments.
The near-field integrals shrink with the far-field fraction; the far-field storage is the
branch-centred row moments (`(lmax + 1)^2` doubles per function pair and branch) plus the much
smaller group moments they are built from. `low_memory=True` drops the branch-centred moments and
re-translates each group in every pass instead, which gives the same numbers to rounding.

Icosane C20H42 / def2-SVP (plan level, one `gamma` and one `J` pass, 4 cores):

| | stored integrals | far-field moments | total | gamma | J |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DF_algo=11` | 0.582 GB | - | 0.582 GB | 0.0116 s | 0.0144 s |
| `DF_algo=12` | 0.276 GB | 0.089 GB | 0.365 GB | 0.0147 s | 0.0127 s |
| `DF_algo=12`, `low_memory=True` | 0.276 GB | 0.005 GB | 0.281 GB | 0.0177 s | 0.0155 s |

So `low_memory` costs about 20% on the per-iteration Coulomb work and takes the saving against
algorithm 11 from 37% to 52%. It only affects the far field, so it does nothing on a compact
molecule, and it does not change the near-field `max_memory_ints3c2e` budget, which still applies
and can be combined with it.

## 6. Limitations and possible extensions

* RI exact exchange (HF, hybrid functionals) needs the complete three-center blocks, so those
  runs use `DF_algo=11` instead (chosen automatically). `low_memory=True` is CPU only and is
  rejected on the GPU.
* The branch <-> atom coupling is a single level (`n_branches x n_atoms` pairs). A box
  hierarchy on the auxiliary side would make it linear scaling for systems with several
  hundred atoms; for the molecules above it is not the bottleneck.
* The two-center metric (`rys_2c2e_symm`, 1.1 s for cholesterol's 2260 auxiliary
  functions) is a fixed cost of both algorithms.
* Analytical gradients (`DFT_Grad`) use their own three-center derivative integrals and
  are not affected.
* The two-center metric is now a large share of the remaining setup cost (2.8 s of tetracontane's
  4.5 s of integral work, against 1.7 s for the whole near-field three-center build). It is a
  per-function Rys kernel that every DF algorithm pays in full, so a shell-blocked version would
  be the next worthwhile target, independently of the far field.
